## What ZigDash is
- ZigDash is an Android app that shows and controls the Zigbee devices of a Zigbee2MQTT installation: a dashboard, device controls, widgets, Quick Settings tiles, alerts, automations and a wall-tablet mode.
- It talks to Zigbee2MQTT over MQTT. It needs an MQTT broker (usually Mosquitto) that Zigbee2MQTT publishes to, or a Home Assistant sign-in when Zigbee2MQTT runs there.
- No cloud, no account. Setup happens at home, with the phone on the same Wi-Fi as the hub.
- Free: setup, dashboards, every device control, widgets, Quick Settings tiles, alerts, three automations per home, the wall tablet. Pro is a one-time purchase (no subscription): unlimited automations, 7- and 30-day history, wall-tablet extras, and remote control through the ZigDash relay. Nothing about connecting a hub is behind Pro.

## Help inside the app
- Get help has a Send button that reaches the developer directly ("Talk to a person" under the assistant's answer). Every message gets an answer, usually within 3 days, in English or Hebrew.
- "Did this help? No" sends the answer to the developer too, with a note on what was wrong.
- ZigDash never asks for passwords, tokens or remote access to a network. Support details hold no addresses, names or logins.
- The setup screen "Nothing yet" opens a step-by-step hub guide for people who have no hub.

## The three kinds of hub
1. SMLIGHT SMHUB: a small box with Zigbee2MQTT and the MQTT broker built in. Its web page is smhub.local (smhub.local:3000 on early hubs); the Dashboard shows its address under Network › IPv4. MQTT is port 1883. In the page, Settings › MQTT has Allow External (lets other devices such as the phone connect) and Allow Anonymous (no login needed). Zigbee2MQTT is started under Apps. An SLZB adapter (SLZB-06, SLZB-MR) is only the Zigbee radio: Zigbee2MQTT and the broker run on another machine.
2. A Raspberry Pi, server, NAS or Docker running Zigbee2MQTT and Mosquitto. Mosquitto 2 listens only on its own machine until mosquitto.conf has "listener 1883" and either "allow_anonymous true" or a password file. In Docker, the broker's port must be published (ports: "1883:1883") and the phone uses the machine's LAN address, never 127.0.0.1 or localhost.
3. Home Assistant with the Mosquitto broker app and the Zigbee2MQTT app (Settings › Apps). ZigDash can sign in with the Home Assistant login and talk to Zigbee2MQTT through it, at home or away, with no broker settings. Home Assistant Container (Docker) has no Apps: there Mosquitto runs in its own container and the broker address is the machine's.
- ZHA (Zigbee Home Automation, Home Assistant's own Zigbee) has no MQTT and ZigDash cannot use it. The only way is to move the devices to Zigbee2MQTT; ZigDash cannot do that.

## The hub guides in the app (what they tell people to do)
- SMHUB: get it · plug it into the router · open its page · let ZigDash in (Settings › MQTT: Allow External, and Allow Anonymous or copy the login) · start Zigbee2MQTT under Apps · pair a device · Find my hub.
- Home Assistant: get a Zigbee adapter · install the Mosquitto broker app · install the Zigbee2MQTT app · pair a device · sign in from ZigDash.
- Raspberry Pi or Linux box: get a Zigbee adapter · install Zigbee2MQTT and Mosquitto with Zigbee2MQTT's own guide · pair a device · Find my hub.
- These are the only configuration changes the assistant may suggest. Never suggest editing Zigbee2MQTT's configuration files by hand beyond them.

## SMHUB warning
- On an SMHUB, Zigbee2MQTT's configuration is generated by the hub. Editing configuration.yaml by hand, or starting Zigbee2MQTT from the Apps page after a hand edit, regenerates the file and breaks the setup. Change Zigbee2MQTT settings on the hub only through the hub's own pages or Zigbee2MQTT's Settings page.

## Why a scan finds nothing (most common first)
- The phone is on a guest Wi-Fi, or the router keeps devices apart (client or AP isolation): the scan sees almost no other hosts.
- A VPN is on: it hides the home network unless the VPN app allows local network (LAN) access.
- The phone is on mobile data, or on a different Wi-Fi than the hub (second router, mesh node in bridge mode, IoT network).
- The broker is bound to localhost: Mosquitto 2 without "listener 1883", or a Docker container whose port is not published.
- The broker is on another subnet: the hub hangs off a second router, or the network is larger than the /24 the scan starts with (the app offers "Look on the whole network").
- There is no hub at all, or Zigbee2MQTT is not set up yet: six in ten setup starts have no hub. Then the hub guide is the answer, not a network fix.
- An SMLIGHT hub with Allow External off is found but refuses the phone.

## Addresses the app understands
- A broker: an IP address like 192.168.1.20, port 1883 (8883 with TLS). 8080 and 80 are web pages, not MQTT.
- Zigbee2MQTT's own page: 192.168.1.20:8080. ZigDash asks the page where its broker is.
- Home Assistant: 192.168.1.20:8123 at home (port 80 on recent Home Assistant OS), or a remote address: https://….ui.nabu.casa, myhome.duckdns.org:8123, or an own domain behind Cloudflare. Remote addresses work from anywhere; a local address needs the same Wi-Fi.
- Names ending in .local often do not work on Android; use the IP address. Tailscale: use the 100.x.y.z address, not the .ts.net name.

## No hub yet

What the "Nothing yet" screen says.

- ZigDash needs one small box at home running Zigbee2MQTT, the free software that talks to Zigbee devices. Pick one of the three ways below, pair your devices on its page, and ZigDash builds the dashboard. No cloud, no account.
- **SMLIGHT SMHUB: the easy start** — A small box with Zigbee2MQTT and the broker built in. Plug it into your router and pair on its web page. Nothing to install.
- **A Raspberry Pi or any Linux box** — Install Zigbee2MQTT and the Mosquitto broker yourself. About an hour, with Zigbee2MQTT's own guide.
- **Home Assistant** — Add the Mosquitto broker and Zigbee2MQTT add-ons, then sign in from ZigDash with your Home Assistant login.
- A Pi or Home Assistant also needs a Zigbee adapter, such as an SLZB-06 or a Sonoff dongle. The SMHUB has one built in.

## Troubleshooting tips

The tips Get help shows, by situation.

- **Same Wi-Fi as your hub** — Your phone must be on the same network as the hub, not a guest network. Turn mobile data off while you set up.
- **The broker is running** — Home Assistant: the Mosquitto broker app is started. Raspberry Pi: Mosquitto is running. SMLIGHT: Settings › MQTT is on.
- **The broker accepts your phone** — SMLIGHT: turn on Allow External. Mosquitto 2 only accepts connections from the hub itself until it's set to listen on the network (port 1883).
- **Mesh Wi-Fi or two routers?** — If the hub hangs off a second router, your phone may not see it. Plug the hub into the main router, or connect by its address.
- **Connect by address** — Find the hub's address in your router's app, then tap Enter details manually.
- **Same network** — Phone and hub must be on the same Wi-Fi. Turn mobile data off.
- **The address changed** — Hubs can get a new address after a restart. Check it in your router's app, and reserve it there so it stays the same.
- **The right port** — MQTT is usually 1883 (8883 with TLS). 8080 or 80 is the hub's web page, not MQTT.
- **The broker is running** — Start Mosquitto or the Mosquitto broker app, then try again.
- **Mosquitto 2** — Mosquitto 2 only accepts connections from the hub itself until it's set to listen on the network (port 1883).
- **The MQTT login, not the web login** — Your hub's web page password is usually not the MQTT one. Home Assistant: use a Home Assistant user, or the login set in the Mosquitto broker app.
- **Check for spaces** — Copying a password can add a space at the end.
- **Zigbee2MQTT uses this broker** — In Zigbee2MQTT's settings, check that its MQTT server is this same broker.
- **Base topic** — If you changed the base topic from "zigbee2mqtt", enter it in manual setup.
- **Pair devices first** — Open Zigbee2MQTT's web page and pair at least one device, then check again.
- **Restart Zigbee2MQTT** — If devices are paired but none arrive, restart Zigbee2MQTT so it publishes its device list.
- **Use the number address** — Names ending in .local don't work on every Android phone. Try the hub's number address, like 192.168.1.20.
- **Port and protocol** — TCP on 1883 is the usual. Pick TLS or WebSocket only if your broker is set up for it.
- **Login** — Leave username and password empty if your broker has none. Otherwise use the MQTT login, not the hub's web login.
- **Is the hub on?** — A power cut or update may have restarted it. Give it a minute after it comes back.
- **Are you at home?** — Away from home, the app needs a remote address (for example Tailscale). Set it in the home's connection settings.
- **Use the Tailscale IP, not the name** — Names ending in .ts.net often don't resolve inside apps. Open the Tailscale app on the phone and make sure it's connected, then use the hub's 100.x.y.z address as the Remote host.
- **Did the address change?** — After a router restart the hub may get a new address. Reserve its address in your router's app, then update the home.
- **Restart Zigbee2MQTT** — If the broker restarted, Zigbee2MQTT's device list is gone until Zigbee2MQTT restarts. Use the Restart Zigbee2MQTT button on the Devices tab.
- **Is Zigbee2MQTT running?** — Open its web page. If it doesn't load, restart it on your hub.
- **Can't connect to my hub** — Same Wi-Fi, broker running, Allow External or Mosquitto listening on the network, then connect by address.
- **What you need** — A small hub running Zigbee2MQTT and an MQTT broker (Mosquitto): an SMLIGHT hub, a Raspberry Pi or any Linux box. Home Assistant works too, but you don't need it.
- **On the same Wi-Fi** — Set up at home, with your phone on the same Wi-Fi as the hub.

## Setup errors

What each setup error means, in the app's words.

- **Zigbee2MQTT's page didn't answer** — Nothing answered as a Zigbee2MQTT page at that address. Open it in this phone's browser to check it, and copy the address exactly (with its port, often 8080). If the page asks for a token, enter your broker details instead.
- **Can't reach this address** — This device and the Zigbee2MQTT host can't reach each other. Check that both are on the same local network.
- **Nothing answers on this port** — The host is reachable, but no MQTT broker answered. Check that the broker is running and that the port is correct.
- **Login required** — This broker needs a username and password.
- **Login rejected** — The broker rejected these credentials.
- **No Zigbee2MQTT here** — An MQTT broker answers here, but Zigbee2MQTT topics weren't found. It may be a different broker.
- **No devices received** — Zigbee2MQTT is running, but no devices were published during the check. Pair devices in Zigbee2MQTT first.
- **Zigbee2MQTT didn't send its device list** — Zigbee2MQTT answers, but its device list didn't come. This usually happens after the MQTT broker restarts: Zigbee2MQTT before 2.14.2 only sends the list again when it restarts. Restart Zigbee2MQTT (or update it), then try again.
- **No Zigbee2MQTT in this Home Assistant** — You're signed in to Home Assistant, but Zigbee2MQTT isn't sending anything on its MQTT. If your Zigbee devices are in ZHA (Home Assistant's built-in Zigbee), ZigDash can't see them: it works with Zigbee2MQTT. If you use the Zigbee2MQTT app, open it under Settings › Apps, check that it's started, then try again.
- **No local network** — Couldn't determine this device's local network. Connect to Wi-Fi and try again.
- **The broker didn't answer the login** — Your broker was found, but it didn't answer the username and password in time. Home Assistant's Mosquitto broker app checks logins with Home Assistant, which can take a few seconds on a Raspberry Pi. Try again, and check that this user exists in Home Assistant. On an SMLIGHT hub, turn on Allow Anonymous under Settings › MQTT instead, and no login is needed.

# Home mode knowledge

For a home that is already connected: using the app, and a home that misbehaves.

## Dashboards and tiles
- Five tabs: **Dashboards**, **Devices**, **Scenes**, **Automations**, **Settings**. A home's own settings: Settings › **Homes** › tap the home. A dashboard's ⋮ menu: **Wall display**, **Add dashboard**, **Export dashboards**, **Import dashboards** (the dashboards' backup).
- Add a tile: **Add tile** on the dashboard lists the home's devices (**Not on a dashboard** first, then **All devices**), plus **Reading** and **Custom MQTT tile**. Pick one, set **Name**, **Section**, **Size** (**Small**, **Wide**, **Full**), tap **Add**. The Devices tab has **Add to a dashboard** per device.
- Edit mode: the pencil (**Edit dashboard**); it does nothing while the dashboard's **Edit lock** is on. It shows **Add tile**, **Add section**, **Dashboard** (its form) and **Done**; tiles get **Move earlier** / **Move later**; sections **Rename section** / **Delete section**.
- Long-press a tile for **Tile options**: **Edit tile** (rename under **Name**, change size), **Move to section**, **Device details**, **Add to home screen**, **Add to Quick Settings**, **Write to NFC tag**, **Remove from dashboard** (with **Undo**).

## The Devices tab
- Every Zigbee2MQTT device, **Needs attention** first; filters **All**, **Needs attention**, **Not on a dashboard**. The tab's dot: new devices not on any dashboard, or a low battery.
- Needs attention: **Battery low** (20% or less), **Offline**, **Not responding**, **Pairing didn't finish**, **Not supported by Zigbee2MQTT**. A **Weak** link is shown but never counted.
- **Not responding**: the device has never sent a state and ZigDash's request for one went unanswered. **Online** / **Offline** come only from Zigbee2MQTT's availability feature, for devices it tracks; ZigDash never guesses them. Availability is turned on in Zigbee2MQTT's own settings, not in ZigDash; **How to turn it on** opens Get help.
- A device that shows wrong or does not respond: check it on Zigbee2MQTT's web page first, then **Link quality** and **Battery** under **Health** on its page. Works in Zigbee2MQTT but not in ZigDash: **Report a problem**.
- Pairing: **Add device** turns on Zigbee2MQTT's permit join ("Pairing — press the device's button", a countdown, **Stop**); then **Add tile**. ZigDash does not remove devices from the network; that happens on Zigbee2MQTT's page.
- Device page: **Rename** (in Zigbee2MQTT itself), **Control**, **Readings** (tap one for **History**), **Health**, **Notify me…** (an alert).

## What the assistant cannot do
- It cannot change any setting, add, rename or move a tile, create an automation, pair, rename or remove a device, restart Zigbee2MQTT, turn on remote control or buy Pro. It names the screen and button that does it.
- It does not see device names, dashboard names, addresses, logins, topics or any device's state. It knows only the facts line it is given (app version, Android, connection kind, Zigbee2MQTT version, bridge state, device count, last error, last scan); it must not claim a device is on, off, online or offline.
- It cannot read Zigbee2MQTT's page or logs, Home Assistant, the broker or the relay.

## Automations and ZigDash Engine

What the Automations tab says, by state.

- **Automations run on your hub, not on your phone** — ZigDash puts ZigDash Engine inside Zigbee2MQTT on your hub. It watches your devices and runs your rules, so a light turns on when the door opens even with your phone off and the internet down.
- **Automations need your MQTT broker** — This home connects through the Zigbee2MQTT page, which is all your dashboards need. Automations, Alerts and history run in the ZigDash Engine on your hub, which talks through your MQTT broker. Add its details once, and ZigDash connects through the broker from then on.
- **Zigbee2MQTT needs one setting changed** — Your Zigbee2MQTT blocks add-ons like ZigDash Engine, as new installs do. To run automations on your hub, ZigDash would turn on "Allow external JavaScript" in Zigbee2MQTT and restart it.
- **Automations paused** — Your hub hasn't reported in for over a minute, so no automation can run until it does. Your rules are kept.
- **Automations paused: ZigDash Engine stopped itself** — It ran into errors three times in an hour and switched off so Zigbee2MQTT keeps running. Your rules are kept.
- Your hub's clock is more than five minutes off. Time-based automations will run at the wrong time until it is fixed.

The Hub backup screen.

- **Restore this backup?** — Zigbee2MQTT's settings, Zigbee network and device list go back to a date. Zigbee2MQTT restarts, which takes about a minute. Devices paired after this backup will need pairing again.
