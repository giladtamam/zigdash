How I replaced my PalGate with a $10 Zigbee relay

Gilad Tamam, the developer of ZigDash · 18 November 2026

Also in: Čeština · Deutsch · Español · Français · עברית · Italiano · Norsk · Nederlands · Polski · Português · Русский · Svenska

My driveway gate opened from the phone through a PalGate Spider-50: a 4G box with its own SIM card that needs a data plan. I took it out and put in a Zigbee relay that cost $10 (about ₪37), once. Now I open the gate from a tile in ZigDash, at home and from anywhere.

About ₪770 for a new Spider-50 in Israeli shops, plus a data SIM to keep renewing, or about ₪37 once for the relay, on the Zigbee hub you already have.

How a phone opens a gate

A gate controller has an open (or start) input: two terminals that open the gate when they are connected together, just like the button on a gate remote. The Spider is a relay with a SIM card. When the PalGate app or a phone call asks it to, it connects those two terminals for a moment.

A Zigbee relay does the same job. The only difference is who tells it to close: your own Zigbee hub at home, instead of a cellular SIM you keep paying for.

What you need

  • A Zigbee dry-contact relay. Mine is a "1 channel Zigbee/WiFi Tuya smart relay, dry contact NO/NC/COM" from AliExpress, about $10. It's sold in WiFi and Zigbee versions: choose Zigbee (the board has a ZT3L module). It is also sold for different supply voltages; I took the AC 85–250V one.
  • Dry contact is the important part. A dry contact is a plain switch with no voltage of its own: it only connects COM to NO, exactly like the button of a remote. A relay that sends voltage out on its terminals (the kind made for switching a lamp) can damage the gate controller's input. Look for "dry contact" and terminals marked COM, NO and NC.
  • A Zigbee2MQTT hub at home that reaches the gate. I already had one (an SMLIGHT SMHUB) for the rest of the house. If you don't, see Hubs.
  • ZigDash on your phone (Google Play or App Store). Opening the gate at home is free. Opening it from anywhere uses ZigDash Pro's remote control: a one-time purchase, no subscription.

1. Pair the relay with ZigDash

Pair it at home first, on the table, before it goes into the gate box.

  1. Power the relay.
  2. In ZigDash, open Devices and tap Start pairing.
  3. Press and hold the relay's button until its LED blinks. That puts it in pairing mode.
  4. ZigDash shows Ready with the new device. Rename it Gate.

Tap it on and off a few times and listen for the relay's click.

2. Take out the Spider

Turn off the gate's power at the breaker first. The gate box has 230V in it. If you're not comfortable working with mains, have an electrician do steps 2 and 3. Don't touch the gate's safety sensors (photocells) or limit switches.
  1. Find the two wires that go from the Spider's relay output (COM1 and N.O.1) to the gate controller's open input. Mark them.
  2. Unscrew the Spider's terminal block, and disconnect the Spider's power.

3. Wire the relay

  1. Output: the two open-input wires go to the relay's COM and NO. Leave NC empty. On a dry contact the order doesn't matter.
  2. Power: the AC 85–250V version takes mains directly, like a phone charger: two wires, live and neutral, from the gate's 230V supply.
  3. Close the relay's case, put it where it stays dry, and turn the breaker back on.
Signal: the gate is usually the farthest point from the hub. In ZigDash, open the relay's device page and check the signal. If it's weak, a mains-powered Zigbee device between the house and the gate (a smart plug, for example) extends the network.

4. A Gate tile in ZigDash

  1. On your dashboard, tap Add from a device and pick Gate.
  2. Tap the tile: the relay closes and the gate starts moving, just as with the remote.
  3. The gate's open input expects a button press, so a contact that stays closed may count as a long press. Check what your gate does, and turn the tile off again if it needs it.
  4. From anywhere: with ZigDash Pro, turn on remote control and the same tile works away from home, without a port opened on your router and with no monthly fee. It covers up to five phones; Share with another phone adds the family's phones with one QR code.

What you give up

To be fair to the Spider, it does things a Zigbee relay doesn't:

PalGate SpiderZigbee relay + ZigDash
CostAbout ₪770 new (shop price), plus a data SIMAbout ₪37 once; remote control is a one-time ZigDash Pro purchase
ConnectionIts own 4G SIM: works when the house has no internetYour Zigbee hub at home; away from home it needs the home internet
Who can openUsers and guests managed in the PalGate app, and opening by phone callPhones you share the home with (up to five remotely)
CloudPalGate's serviceNone at home; remote goes through the ZigDash relay, end-to-end encrypted

For a private house where the family opens one gate, the relay does everything I needed. For a building with many residents and visitors, a managed service like PalGate still makes sense.
